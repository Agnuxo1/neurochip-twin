NeuroChip Twin v2 is a few-shot digital twin of neural network development on a multi-electrode-array chip. From a handful of measured concentrations of a chemical it has never seen, it forecasts how all 17 network features evolve across the whole dose range and developmental time, says how sure it is, calls developmental-neurotoxicity hazard and tells the lab which cultures can stop at DIV 7.

The problem

Every new compound costs a full dose–response campaign

The bottleneck

Developmental-neurotoxicity testing is moving from animals to in-vitro methods. Its functional anchor is the network formation assay: rat cortical neurons grow on multi-electrode-array plates while exposed to a chemical, and spontaneous activity is recorded as the network forms, at DIV 5, 7, 9 and 12. Each compound needs several concentrations, replicate wells and twelve days of culture. Analysis is one curve per endpoint, with no calibrated uncertainty and no memory of the hundreds of compounds already measured.

Our question

After measuring one to three concentrations of a new compound, can we forecast its full effect on network development (every feature, every day, every concentration) with honest uncertainty, decide whether it is hazardous, and choose what to measure next? A model that answers this is a data-driven simulator that can be queried at conditions that were never run, and that improves with every experiment.

Who it is for

Neural organ-on-a-chip labs that run compartmentalised chips with electrophysiology and want to spend wells and chips on informative conditions, turning recordings into a reusable predictive asset. Toxicologists running the in-vitro developmental-neurotoxicity battery, who need hazard calls with honest uncertainty from fewer wells.

How it works

A chemical is a few-shot task

One model, meta-learned across many chemicals, treats each new chemical as a small task: a few measured concentrations in, the complete dose × day × feature response out.

  1. 1

    Measure a few doses

    Record k concentrations (1 to 3) on the chip at DIV 5, 7, 9 and 12. Anchoring on the top concentration first helps; DoseCompass proposes the next one by expected information.

  2. 2

    Forecast everything

    NeuroTrajectory, a conditional neural process with an interpolation-informed gated decoder and dose-local attention, learns per feature and day how far to trust the measured neighbours.

  3. 3

    Know when to trust it

    Cross-conformal 90 % intervals, stratified by cytotoxicity and number of measured doses, plus an epistemic score that makes the twin abstain (“I don’t know: measure another dose”).

  4. 4

    Decide

    A hazard call from three doses, potency by interpolation of measured doses, the next concentration to test, a DIV 7 stop gate and, on a four-compartment chip, directed propagation between compartments.

Why the band matters

A forecast is only useful if its uncertainty is honest. The ensemble's own (parametric) intervals under-cover slightly; cross-conformal calibration on the other folds brings coverage to the nominal level, and the twin abstains when its members disagree.

Evidence boundaries

What we do not claim

  • Not a twin of a commercial chip. The biology is rat primary cortical cultures on 48-well MEA plates (EPA Network Formation Assay). Transfer to human iPSC-derived networks or to a sponsor's chips is untested.
  • Not a clinical or regulatory decision. Forecasts and hazard calls are research outputs for screening design.
  • Chip layer is descriptive. Propagation parameters come from unstimulated recordings of one four-compartment design, with no pharmacology; any “what-if” built on them is a simulation, not a measurement.
  • Small negative class. Few DNT-negative reference chemicals limit every specificity estimate.
  • Limited baselines. Statistical and nearest-neighbour models, EPA's own rules and the team lead's earlier architectures; no descriptor-based QSAR or Gaussian-process dose–response model was run.
  • Multiplicity. Many intervals and tests are reported without correction; primary endpoints are marked and post-hoc analyses are labelled in the report.

Negative and tied results we report

  • negative Potency derived from the model is worse than interpolating the measured doses, so potency is reported by interpolation.
  • negative Separating specific from cytotoxic disruption did not work (chance-level AUROC).
  • tie DoseCompass ties with a log-spaced design (it beats random designs).
  • not better At DIV 7 the primary twin score does not beat persistence as a throughput gate; its upper band is kept as the safety gate.

The explorer shows one evenly spread design per k and a conformal band stratified by k only (the cytotoxicity strata are not in the public bundle); the report averages several seeded designs per k. Both differences are stated next to the numbers they affect.

Practical value

A workflow for a neural organ-on-a-chip lab

Fewer treated wells per compound become more compounds per plate, or more replicates exactly where the twin abstains. Each finished experiment joins the training asset, so the next compound starts from a better prior.

  1. Describe the experiment in a simple assay and chip schema (compound, concentrations, days, compartments, tunnel directions).
  2. Anchor: measure the top concentration first, then the concentrations DoseCompass proposes.
  3. Forecast every untested concentration and day, with conformal intervals; read the abstention flag.
  4. Stop early: at DIV 7, apply the throughput or the safety gate, chosen by the cost of a missed neurotoxicant.
  5. Decide: hazard call; potency by interpolation of measured doses; if the twin abstains, add a concentration.
  6. Update: retrain the twin in batch with each finished experiment (no online assimilation yet).

Reproducibility

Runs on a laptop CPU, offline

Quickstart

git clone REPO_URL neurochip-twin
cd neurochip-twin
pip install -r requirements.txt

# a held-out forecast on CPU (no network, about a minute)
python demo.py --chemical "Deltamethrin" --k 3

# tests
pytest -q

Rebuild this website

python scripts/precompute_site.py     # site/data from data_bundle/ and results/
python -m http.server 8000 -d site    # open http://localhost:8000
  • Frozen inputs. Cohort, aliases, quality rules, normalisation and splits are frozen by SHA-256 and checked by a script.
  • Registered protocols. The planning and early-exit analyses were written to protocol files and hashed before any result was computed.
  • One source of numbers. Every number in the report, the README, the Writeup and this page is rendered from results/*.json; the build stops on an untraced number.
  • Shipped weights. The cross-validated ensemble ships in data_bundle/, so every forecast here can be re-created exactly.
  • Tests. A pytest suite covers ingestion, the cohort freeze, features, trajectories, the planner, the chip layer and the app core.
